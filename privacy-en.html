Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 25, 2026

Thank you for using Ashiato-cho ("the App").

This page explains what information the App handles, where that information goes, and what never leaves your device. We have kept the legal wording to a minimum.

Who Runs the App

The App is built and run by an independent developer, not by a company.

  • Developer: Deploy (sole proprietor, not incorporated)
  • Contact: kimura0223sota@gmail.com
  • Website: https://kimu0223.github.io/

The developer above is the "controller" under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). There is no Data Protection Officer, so please send everything to the email address above.

Information That Stays on Your Device

The following stays on your device only. It is never sent to a server, and the developer cannot see it.

  • Your pairs (name, brand, model name, category, the day you started wearing them, the day they retired, your star rating, custom order)
  • Your memories (the text, the tag "match, training, milestone, travel, everyday", and the date)
  • Photos (kept in a folder that belongs to the App on your device)
  • App settings (design, palette, sort order)

The App has no accounts and no sign-in. The developer runs no server, so there is no mechanism that could receive any of this.

Removing the App from your device removes all of it, photos included. To remove a single pair, let it go from inside the App. Its photos and memories go with it.

iCloud sync is not offered at this time. If it is ever added, this policy will be updated first.

Photos and EXIF (Location Data)

Photos taken on a phone often carry EXIF data, which can include the GPS coordinates and the time the photo was taken.

The App strips EXIF data out before it saves a photo, so no GPS coordinates are kept.

The photos themselves also stay on your device and are never uploaded.

Advertising

The free version shows ads from Google AdMob (provided by Google LLC, United States). Buying Premium, a one-time purchase, removes them.

To serve those ads, Google may receive information about your device, including the advertising identifier (IDFA). That processing is carried out by Google, not by the developer.

  • The advertising identifier (IDFA) is used for personalized ads only if you allow it on the iOS "Allow tracking" prompt (App Tracking Transparency).
  • If you do not allow it, you see non-personalized ads instead. Ads do not disappear.
  • In the European Economic Area (EEA), the United Kingdom and other regions where consent is required, Google's consent form (User Messaging Platform) appears. Google determines which regions those are.
  • If consent is not given in one of those regions, the App does not request ads at all.
  • In regions where the consent form appears, a "Privacy options" row appears in the App's Settings. You can change your choices there at any time.

Screens showing only Legacy (retired) pairs never show ads.

How Usage Is Analyzed

To learn which features get used and make the App better, the App sends usage data to PostHog (provided by PostHog Inc., United States; the endpoint is us.i.posthog.com).

In the European Economic Area (EEA), the United Kingdom and other regions where consent is required, the analytics component is not even started until you consent, and nothing at all is sent. You can withdraw that consent at any time from "Privacy options" in Settings, which stops any further data being sent.

What the App sends is only the fact that something happened:

  • A pair was added, moved to Legacy, or moved back to Active
  • A memory was added
  • A brand was picked from the list or typed in, or a sample color was chosen
  • A tab, the timeline, or the memories list was opened
  • A design or palette was changed, or could not be changed on the free version
  • The Premium screen was opened, a purchase went through, or a video ad was watched to the end

The only values sent alongside those are ones that cannot identify you: the tab name, the design and palette identifiers, counts, days worn, the screen name, and which input method was used.

The names of your pairs, the text of your memories, and your photos are never sent.

On top of that, the PostHog SDK attaches the following by default:

  • A random, anonymous ID created for your device (it is not tied to a name or an email address)
  • A record of the App being installed, updated, opened, and sent to the background
  • The name of the screen being shown
  • Device model, OS name and version, app version, language, time zone, screen size, and network type
  • Because of how network requests work, your IP address reaches PostHog's servers

Session replay (screen recording) is not used.

Purchases

Premium purchases and restores are handled by Apple (the App Store and StoreKit).

Payment details such as card numbers never reach the App. All the App receives is whether a purchase exists.

Why We Process Data, and on What Legal Basis

Here is what each kind of processing is for, and which basis in Article 6 of the GDPR it relies on.

  • Running the App itself (recording and showing your pairs and memories, saving photos): performance of our contract with you (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)). This data is handled on your device only.
  • Reflecting Premium purchases and restores: performance of our contract with you (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)).
  • Showing personalized ads: your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a)), given through the iOS tracking prompt and, in the regions concerned, Google's consent form.
  • Showing non-personalized ads so the free version can stay free: the developer's legitimate interests (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). In regions where consent is required, no ads are requested without it.
  • Analyzing usage to improve the App: in regions where consent is required (the EEA, the UK and the like), your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a)) — no analytics run without it. Elsewhere, the developer's legitimate interests in finding bugs and making features better (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). Either way, only anonymous data that cannot identify you is involved. You can object to this processing at any time.

Using the App does not require you to provide a name, an email address, a phone number or anything similar. Declining tracking does not limit any feature; it only means ads are not tailored to you.

The App does not carry out automated decision-making that produces legal effects concerning you or similarly significantly affects you.

Who Receives Your Data

The developer does not sell your data. It goes only as far as it has to for the services below to work.

  • Google LLC (United States) — advertising. Privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • How Google uses information from apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites
  • PostHog Inc. (United States) — usage analytics. Privacy policy: https://posthog.com/privacy
  • Apple Inc. (United States) — app distribution and purchases. Privacy policy: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/

Apart from disclosures required by law, your data goes nowhere else.

Transfers to the United States

Google and PostHog are both United States companies, so data reaches servers outside your own country.

Both state that they use mechanisms such as the EU Standard Contractual Clauses for these transfers. Which mechanism applies in practice is set by each company, so please see their privacy policies linked above for the details.

How Long Data Is Kept

  • Data on your device (pairs, memories, photos, settings): kept until you remove the App from your device, and gone the moment you do.
  • Data sent to Google and PostHog: kept for as long as each company's own policy provides. Please see their privacy policies for the details.

Your Rights

You have the following rights over your personal data. If you live in the EU, the EEA or the UK these come from Articles 15 to 22 of the GDPR; if you live in Japan they come from the Act on the Protection of Personal Information.

  • The right to access your data
  • The right to have mistakes corrected
  • The right to erasure
  • The right to restrict processing
  • The right to object to processing
  • The right to receive your data in a portable form (data portability)
  • The right to withdraw consent at any time

For the data on your device, you can view, edit and erase it yourself from inside the App. The developer does not hold this data and therefore cannot retrieve or erase it on your behalf.

For data sent to Google and PostHog, please write to the email address above and the developer will pass your request on as far as he is able. Because that data is tied only to an anonymous ID, it may not be possible to identify the records that are yours (GDPR Article 11).

Withdrawing Consent and Turning Tracking Off

  • Open the iOS Settings app, go to Privacy and Security, then Tracking, and you can turn this App's permission off at any time. Once it is off, ads are no longer personalized.
  • In regions where the consent form appears, you can change your choices at any time from "Privacy options" in the App's Settings.
  • Buying Premium, a one-time purchase, removes ads.
  • Removing the App from your device erases the data on it and stops any further data from being sent.

Withdrawing consent does not make processing that already took place unlawful.

Children Under 13

The App is built for athletes, mainly high school and university students.

The App does not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you are a parent or guardian and find that your child has been using the App, please write to the email address above.

If a change materially affects you, it will also be noted in the App's release notes.
